# Literature supporting the architecture argument

The [manuscript](../../paper/manuscript_draft.md) compares provider preparation and product order with eligible terminal derivation. This reference records why the cited work belongs in that argument. It distinguishes methodological precedents, technical precedents, and public context from evidence produced by this repository. Literature does not calibrate the scene sizes, terminal rates, deadlines, or processing times in the [current configuration](../../results/current/config.json).

## Review scope and source access

The review was conducted on September 30, 2026. It began with the draft's references and followed four questions: how architecture models connect to simulation; where useful-product latency begins and ends; how onboard preparation and download planning differ; and what interrupted delivery means at the receiver. Searches used the cited titles and combinations of Earth observation, architecture trade, onboard processing, product latency, download scheduling, progressive transmission, and intermittent delivery. New sources were selected for their direct relevance to those questions. Research claims rely on publishers, author institutions, or government repositories; protocol claims rely on the issuing bodies.

This is a targeted review, not a systematic review of every satellite architecture or scheduling paper. Search coverage is insufficient to support a first-of-its-kind claim. The table records the portion of each primary source inspected. In particular, Tonetti's institutional abstract and publisher summary support the broad comparison below; detailed quantitative results are not borrowed from that paper. Ryan's scanned proceedings paper was read visually. No paywalled or inaccessible result is treated as if its full text had been reviewed.

The rendered-manuscript review checked reference numbering and the architecture claims against this synthesis. Public program descriptions remain context, hardware papers remain precedents for their own implementations, and protocol standards remain conceptual foundations. The Army announcement is attributed to the U.S. Army, retaining its March 2024 publication date and current official access link. The draft abstract draws its findings from the repository's claim filters; the submitted abstract remains the historical record.

## What the comparison adds

The relevant distinction is how the architecture and the evidence treat the requested product at receipt. The [catalogs](../../model/) state the assumed need, product coverage and detail, common function owners, and candidate-specific execution modes. The [transfer model](../../src/leo_edge/simulation.py) records product byte progress and completion. The [request evaluation](../../experiments/e11_mission_thread_success.py) then adds eligible terminal derivation and compares sufficient availability with the request deadline. This combination makes the reasoning inspectable without treating transmission alone as completion of the user's need.

Three distinctions organize the argument. First, request-to-collection delay remains visible rather than being absorbed into a post-acquisition latency measure. Second, complete receipt and sufficient availability differ when the terminal must derive the requested view. Third, timely sufficiency and full-scene receipt answer separate requirements. The principal corridor case demonstrates why these distinctions matter, but its numerical outcomes belong to the [paired trial evidence](../../results/current/single_request_trials.csv), not to the cited literature.

The provider-versus-terminal decision is correspondingly bounded. Provider preparation can place a fitting product earlier in a contact, while terminal derivation waits for an eligible complete scene. Both remain subject to collection and contact timing. Tasking and collection ownership are fixed, and priority is evaluated as product ordering within one request. The supported contribution is a traceable application and conditional comparison with reproducible evidence. A claim of a new allocation theory, scheduling algorithm, or delivery protocol would exceed the work.

## How the sources constrain interpretation

Public program descriptions motivate the user boundary. They do not authorize replacing synthetic inputs with implied operational requirements. Hardware studies show that processing belongs in the delivery chain, but their measurements apply to their implementations and workloads. Scheduling papers address shared constraints that this study deliberately does not simulate. Compression and transport standards provide established meanings that help avoid overstating what A4, A6, or byte carryover implements. Each source therefore narrows the claim as well as supporting it.

The numerical comparison should be read under assumed product sufficiency. Native spatial resolution, declared coverage, complete encoded bytes, and remaining terminal processing time are model conditions. They do not establish image quality or human interpretation. A future empirical study would need to test those conditions with representative imagery and measured implementations before generalizing the architecture implication. The existing [verification page](../V_AND_V.md) keeps that validation boundary separate from internal correctness.
